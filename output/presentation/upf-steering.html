UPF Load Steering — Live Closed Loop

Reading C-DOT's Prometheus every 30 s, re-optimising every 10 min, and writing per-UPF weights back through the SMF API.

Carried load per UPF

Each line is one UPF's total N3 throughput. The shaded region is when the loop could actually write to the SMF — before that a relay outage on our host blocked the write path, so the loop solved but held.

Imbalance between UPFs

Spread between the busiest and quietest UPF, as a percentage of the average. Lower is better — a perfectly balanced network sits at 0%. This is the single number the optimiser is trying to push down.

Peak load removed, per control cycle

How much the solve took off the busiest UPF, measured against leaving the weights unchanged. The first cycle after the write path recovered found the network badly skewed; later cycles found progressively less to correct.

Weights the optimiser wrote, per traffic group

The core output of the loop. Each panel is one (DNN, TAC) group; the bands show what share of that group's traffic each UPF was told to take. Every panel sums to 100 at all times. Flat stretches mean the solver saw no reason to move traffic; steps are re-allocations. Ticks under a panel mark the cycles that actually reached the SMF.

Latest weights as a table

How much the allocation moved each cycle

Total weight shifted between UPFs, in weight-points, from one cycle to the next. Large early moves are the optimiser correcting a skewed network; small later moves are it holding a good allocation against drifting traffic. A controller that thrashes would show a flat high line here.

Forecast vs. what actually arrived

The forecaster predicts each group's demand 10 minutes ahead; the solver allocates against that prediction. Solid line is what actually arrived, dashed is what was predicted for that moment. The closer the two lines, the better the allocation was aimed. Green markers are held points — the model returned zero for a group that had been busy, so the solver parked it at its recent level rather than dropping it.

How far off were the forecasts?

Every modelled prediction, bucketed by how far it missed. Held points are excluded — they are not predictions. The tall left-hand bar is the story: most forecasts land within a couple of percent. The grey bars are the misses — a real tail worth showing rather than cropping.

Telemetry availability

Every 30-second poll of C-DOT's Prometheus, coloured by whether it answered. The gap is a relay failure on our own host, not a fault on their side — worth showing, because it is what the loop's hold-and-recover behaviour was built for.
